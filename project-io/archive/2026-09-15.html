The permit boundary moves to the foreground.

Published September 15, 2026 · 8:36 AM MDT

ORANGE · PERMIT-BOUNDARY WATCH

Oracle now formally argues that construction underway on the data-center campus is legally and physically separate from Yucca Growth Infrastructure’s adjacent proposed microgrid, whose air-permit proceeding remains stayed. The distinction is material, but Oracle’s compliance statement is a party position—not a court or NMED finding.

Verified and attributable developments

Oracle draws a facility boundary

In a September 14 statement, Oracle said the data-center campus and microgrid occupy separate adjacent sites, have separate operators, and are separate facilities. Oracle says construction to date is on the data-center campus under county permits and does not require the microgrid’s air permit. It says the Supreme Court stay applies to the administrative proceeding for air permit 10883, not to county-authorized data-center building work.

Oracle further says YGI has discussed microgrid-site preconstruction activities with NMED and is following agency guidance. No written NMED concurrence, inspection record, site plan or activity log supporting that assertion was located in this review.

Permit remains pending

NMED’s daily permitting dashboard, updated September 14 at 9:00 PM, continued to list YGI’s Microgrid, agency interest 41351 and NSR permit 10883, among actions under review. The dashboard says issued, denied or withdrawn actions leave the list on its next update; continued listing supports a pending status, not approval or denial.

Applicant-retained expert enters the record

Oracle separately publicized comments by environmental-health scientist Christopher Ollson supporting the revised fuel-cell plan. Oracle discloses that YGI retained him. The cited review says the redesign would reduce nitrogen oxides about 92 percent versus the earlier turbine-and-diesel design, avoid diesel backup generators, require an approximately 11-million-gallon one-time non-potable fill, and use about 168,000 gallons annually for maintenance. Those are applicant-sponsored expert conclusions, not agency findings.

Allegations, disputed positions and limits

Oracle asserts that YGI is operating in compliance with New Mexico law. That assertion has not been adopted in a published merits order or NMED determination found here. The proper factual test is not whether “Jupiter construction” is occurring in general, but what work is occurring on which parcel, for which operator, and whether any microgrid source or emitting equipment is being constructed.

The expert’s 92 percent figure compares the revised design with the withdrawn turbine-and-diesel design. It does not establish zero emissions, zero health impact or superiority to every alternative. The water figures concern microgrid and data-center systems, not disputed construction-phase water pumping. No evidence reviewed establishes misconduct or corruption.

Analytical judgment

Today’s legal boundary is plausible and consequential, but it is document-sensitive. Entity labels and adjacent parcels do not alone resolve whether physical work belongs to the data center or the regulated source. The most probative records are the site plan, parcel ownership and control, county permits, contractor scopes, dated photographs, equipment-delivery logs, NMED guidance and inspection notes.

The expert release strengthens YGI’s technical advocacy while also clarifying provenance: an expert retained by the applicant is different from the independent third-party environmental assessments Oracle has promised after operations begin.
