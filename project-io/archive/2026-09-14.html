Daily intelligence brief · Public-safe edition

The grid boundary needs four ledgers.

Published September 14, 2026 · 8:39 AM MDT

ORANGE · GRID-BOUNDARY WATCH

A newly indexed claim-by-claim review of the September 8 county tape adds a useful distinction: Oracle described existing El Paso Electric service for construction and house/backup power while pursuing an EPE easement. That is different from using the grid for the future 2.45 GW operating load, which remains a contingency rather than an executed plan.

Verified and attributable developments

Present service versus future operating power

A civic fact-check built from the county’s September 8 meeting tape and timestamped auto-caption transcript records Oracle saying it is buying El Paso Electric power for construction and house/backup service. The same review records discussion of an EPE easement, which a commissioner characterized as perpetual. PROJECT IO treats the transcript as attributable meeting evidence; the executed utility-service and easement instruments were not located.

Oracle’s April announcement still states that up to 2.45 GW of Bloom fuel cells will fully power the completed campus. Oracle also told commissioners that if the air permit fails it would need to work with a grid provider, with the form of that arrangement still to be determined. These statements describe separate present and potential future uses.

Material change from September 12

The earlier brief identified grid power only as a fallback for operations. The county-tape review shows the project already has a narrower utility relationship for construction and backup loads and is pursuing a land instrument connected to EPE. This narrows the question; it does not establish that the data-center operating load will be placed on the public grid.

Allegations, disputed positions and limits

Project opponents argue the utility relationship conflicts with “independent power” messaging. Oracle’s public materials describe an independently powered completed campus and say Oracle will bear its energy costs. Construction service, house/backup power and full operating load are not interchangeable, so the statements are in tension only if their scopes are blurred.

No executed easement, interconnection application, load study or cost-allocation agreement was located. No evidence reviewed establishes hidden full-load grid service, customer cost shifting, misconduct or corruption.

Analytical judgment

Power claims now require the same discipline as water claims. Maintain four distinct ledgers: construction service; house/backup service; full operating load; renewable-energy matching. A single phrase such as “off-grid” or “grid-connected” is too coarse to describe Jupiter accurately.

The highest-value document is the proposed EPE easement. Its legal description, term, permitted facilities, capacity, assignment rights, cost responsibility and relationship to any interconnection request would show whether it is routine construction service, enduring backup infrastructure or preparation for a larger operating option.

Actor and issue ledger

  • Oracle: meeting statements distinguish current limited utility service from a possible future grid arrangement.
  • El Paso Electric: identified as present construction/backup supplier and easement counterparty; no full-load commitment located.
  • Bloom / Yucca: proposed 2.45 GW fuel-cell system and contested air permit remain the primary operating path.
  • Doña Ana County: commissioners questioned easement duration and previously identified possible agreement amendments for a power-source change.
  • STACK / BorderPlex: no new public record changed their known development roles.
  • Water, courts, IRB and lobbying: no new meter ledger, merits order, pipeline approval, IRB ruling or lobbying disclosure located.

Indicators requiring follow-up

  1. Executed or draft EPE easement, legal description, route map and facilities schedule.
  2. Construction-service and house/backup-service agreements, meters, demand limits and payer.
  3. Any full-load interconnection request, system-impact study or rate-treatment proposal.
  4. County legal analysis of whether existing limited service differs from the agreement trigger described September 8.
  5. Supreme Court orders, the August 31 water report and complete OSE meter history.
