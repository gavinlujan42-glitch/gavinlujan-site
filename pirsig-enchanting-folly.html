BRIEF 08 · PIRSIG + THE HUMAN JOURNEY

Quality and the Enchanting Folly

The human journey is an unfinished machine: beautiful, unreliable, and forever asking us to care about the work.

GAVIN LUJAN · NEW MEXICO · AUGUST 2026

Robert Pirsig's inquiry into Quality still haunts the modern enterprise because it names something our metrics cannot fully hold. We measure uptime, velocity, findings, cost, and throughput. We should. But everybody who has practiced a craft knows that quality appears before the spreadsheet can explain it.

The machine and the rider

A system can pass every test and still feel wrong. A team can hit every milestone while losing the meaning of the work. The classical view sees structure; the romantic view experiences the whole. Leadership fails when it chooses one and mocks the other.

I want the architecture diagram and the conversation with the person doing the work. I want the control evidence and the story of what happens at 4:45 on a Friday when the process breaks. Quality lives where those perspectives meet.

Our enchanting folly

Human beings keep building permanence with temporary hands. We cast concrete in the desert, launch instruments toward the stars, encode memory in machines, argue about truth, fall in love, lose our passwords, and begin again. It is folly, but enchanting folly.

The answer is not cynicism. It is care. Maintain the machine. Teach the apprentice. Question the requirement. Repair what can be repaired. Keep enough humor to survive the gap between design and reality. Quality is not perfection. It is the living trace of attention—and perhaps the best evidence that we were here.

The operating principle

Protect the mission. Enable the people. Preserve human authority. Build what is worthy of the future.

FIELD NOTE

Written from a New Mexican executive perspective where technology, public service, craft, and human consequence meet.
