BRIEF 12 · AIKIDO + CYBER RESILIENCE

Centered
Under Pressure

Meet force without surrendering judgment. Preserve the center, connect to reality, and redirect energy toward protection and recovery.

GAVIN LUJAN · ZIA WOLF SECURITY · AUGUST 15, 2026

Aikido is not a cybersecurity technique and should not be reduced to a corporate metaphor. Its ethical and physical practice is its own discipline. Yet its emphasis on center, awareness, entering, circular movement, and harmony offers a useful lens for leaders who must operate amid conflict without becoming consumed by it.

Center before motion

During an incident, the first threat to leadership is often cognitive: urgency collapses perspective, rumor outruns evidence, and people mistake movement for progress. A centered response establishes the mission, incident authority, shared facts, decision rhythm, and communication channel. This is organizational posture.

Center is not passivity. It is the stable point from which decisive action becomes possible. The incident commander can contain systems, revoke access, preserve evidence, invoke continuity plans, and notify leadership because roles and priorities were established before pressure arrived.

Connect to the force

Aikido training emphasizes relationship to movement rather than rigid collision. Cyber defenders likewise need contact with the actual event: telemetry, identity logs, endpoint evidence, network flows, system owner knowledge, and adversary behavior. We do not respond to the imagined incident. We connect to what is occurring and update the response as evidence changes.

Redirect, do not merely resist

Resilience converts hostile energy into learning and stronger design. A phishing attempt becomes a test of identity controls and reporting culture. Ransomware pressure becomes proof of segmentation, recovery, and executive decision readiness. A vendor compromise becomes a reason to improve trust relationships, contractual evidence, and dependency mapping.

The goal is not to romanticize attack. Harm is real. The goal is to prevent the attack from dictating every choice. Recovery, transparency, and learning restore agency.

Harmony requires boundaries

Harmony is not appeasement. Healthy systems have boundaries: least privilege, separation of duties, strong authentication, segmented networks, protected backups, explicit approval, and consequences for unsafe behavior. Compassion for people can coexist with firmness about controls. In fact, clear boundaries protect teams from improvising under impossible conditions.

  • Center: establish mission, authority, facts, and cadence.
  • Enter: move toward decisive evidence and the point of consequence.
  • Blend: coordinate technology, legal, communications, operations, and leadership.
  • Redirect: contain harm, preserve service, and turn lessons into design.
Calm is not the absence of urgency. It is disciplined attention in the presence of urgency.

Weekend action

Run a 30-minute no-fault incident drill. Give the team an ambiguous alert and one business consequence. Ask only four questions: Who is in command? What do we know? What must we protect first? When is the next decision point? That practice builds the center before it is needed.
