Trust Center

This center describes the principles intended to govern GavinLujan.com and future secure client services. It is an operational starting point, not legal advice or a substitute for counsel reviewing production policies.

Privacy

The public site is designed to minimize collection of personal information. Information voluntarily submitted by email or through a future portal should be used only for the stated business purpose, protected according to its sensitivity, retained only as needed, and not sold.

AI Transparency

Artificial intelligence may assist research, drafting, analysis, prototyping, and knowledge workflows. Material client deliverables should remain subject to human review. Confidential information should not be submitted to public AI services without authorization and appropriate safeguards.

Security Practices

  • Encrypted transport using HTTPS.
  • Least-privilege access and role-based authorization.
  • Multi-factor authentication for protected services.
  • Secure development, dependency review, logging, backups, and incident response.
  • Vendor and data-flow review before processing confidential information.

Security concerns may be reported to gavinlujan@gmail.com. Please do not include sensitive exploit details in an initial unencrypted message.

Terms and Acceptable Use

Public content is informational. Access to restricted areas may require a separate agreement, NDA, verified account, and acceptance of project-specific terms. Users must not attempt unauthorized access, disrupt services, scrape protected information, introduce malicious code, or misuse content or credentials.

Accessibility and Respect

The site aims to support accessible navigation, readable contrast, semantic structure, and inclusive communication. Its Southwest visual language uses original abstractions inspired by landscape and natural systems. It does not claim ceremonial authority or reproduce protected tribal, religious, or governmental symbols.

Policy Development Notice

Before collecting accounts, signatures, payments, confidential documents, analytics identifiers, or regulated information, these policies should be expanded and reviewed for the actual technology stack, jurisdictions, vendors, and business practices involved.
