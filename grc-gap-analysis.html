GOVERNANCE · RISK · COMPLIANCE · ASSURANCE

GRC Gap Analysis:
from compliance theater to evidence.

A gap analysis should not become a spreadsheet graveyard. It should expose where mission objectives, obligations, controls, evidence, ownership, and actual operating reality diverge, then convert those differences into a prioritized remediation program.

Operating principle: a control is not mature because a policy says it exists. It is mature when ownership is clear, implementation is observable, evidence is repeatable, exceptions are governed, and leadership understands the residual risk.

THE MODEL

Six lenses for finding the real gap.

01 · Governance

Decision rights, accountability, policy hierarchy, risk appetite, committees, exceptions, and executive reporting.

02 · Risk

Asset and business context, threat scenarios, likelihood, impact, treatment, acceptance, dependencies, and residual risk.

03 · Controls

Administrative, technical, and physical safeguards mapped to requirements and implemented at the correct scope.

04 · Evidence

Logs, configurations, tickets, approvals, tests, inventories, attestations, metrics, and other proof that controls operate.

05 · Compliance

Applicable laws, contracts, policies, standards, regulatory duties, audit commitments, and customer obligations.

06 · Resilience

Detection, response, recovery, continuity, third-party failure, cyber exercises, lessons learned, and measurable improvement.

REFERENCE STACK

Crosswalk once. Reuse everywhere.

Build a common control spine instead of treating every framework as a separate universe. The exact obligations depend on the organization, but a practical enterprise baseline can map controls across NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53, NIST RMF, NIST AI RMF, CIS Controls, Zero Trust principles, privacy requirements, contractual obligations, and sector-specific rules such as CJIS, HIPAA, PCI DSS, or FedRAMP where applicable.

Control objective first.

Describe the security or governance outcome in plain language before mapping framework identifiers.

Many-to-one mapping.

One well-designed control and evidence package can satisfy multiple overlapping requirements.

Scope is explicit.

Identify systems, data, identities, vendors, facilities, AI models, agents, and business processes covered.

Evidence has a clock.

Record evidence owner, collection method, freshness, retention, test frequency, and source of truth.

ASSESSMENT ENGINE

Score the gap, not the paperwork.

For each control objective, capture: requirement → current state → evidence → effectiveness → gap → risk → owner → remediation → due date → validation.

Maturity scale: 0 = absent; 1 = ad hoc; 2 = documented; 3 = implemented; 4 = measured; 5 = continuously improved.

Evidence confidence: None / Anecdotal / Partial / Repeatable / Independently verifiable.

Priority: combine mission impact, exploitability or failure likelihood, exposure, compliance consequence, control dependency, and remediation urgency. Never let a numeric score erase executive judgment.

90-DAY REMEDIATION ROADMAP

Stabilize. Prove. Improve.

0–30 Days · Stabilize

Confirm scope and obligations. Name control owners. Identify crown jewels. Close intolerable exposures. Establish the gap register and executive risk acceptance path.

31–60 Days · Prove

Collect evidence, test key controls, reconcile inventories, validate identity and recovery controls, tier third parties, and define AI governance boundaries.

61–90 Days · Improve

Automate evidence where practical, measure effectiveness, retire duplicate controls, exercise response and recovery, and publish a funded risk-based roadmap.

The goal is not to become compliant on paper. The goal is to know what must be true, prove what is true, expose what is not, and govern the difference.

EXECUTIVE OUTPUT

The one-page GRC view leadership needs.

Top enterprise risks

Five to ten material scenarios, trend direction, exposure, treatment status, and accountable owner.

Control health

Coverage, effectiveness, evidence confidence, overdue exceptions, and critical dependencies.

Compliance posture

Material obligations, known deficiencies, audit commitments, deadlines, and accepted exceptions.

Remediation economics

What gets funded first, why it reduces risk, dependencies, target dates, and the risk of delay.

GRC becomes valuable when it stops being a library of requirements and becomes a navigation system for executive decisions.
