An executive-priority assessment of state-sponsored espionage and disruption groups, financially motivated criminal ecosystems, and private-sector offensive actors. Ranked for strategic relevance to U.S. public-sector and New Mexico environments, not as a universal league table.

EXECUTIVE JUDGMENT: New Mexico's distinctive risk is convergence. State government, water, energy, telecommunications, healthcare, universities, national-laboratory supply chains, defense contractors and a growing compute ecosystem create overlapping intelligence, disruption and extortion incentives. The most consequential actors are therefore not necessarily the loudest ransomware brands.

Actor Notes

01 · PRC · STATE/CONTRACTOR ECOSYSTEM

Salt Typhoon

High-end telecommunications espionage. U.S. and allied reporting describes PRC-linked actors compromising backbone and edge infrastructure, preserving long-term access and using trusted network relationships to expand collection. For New Mexico, telecom compromise is an upstream risk multiplier: government, labs, healthcare and private firms all inherit exposure through communications dependencies.

02 · PRC · NATION-STATE

Volt Typhoon

Designed around stealthy persistence in U.S. critical infrastructure, frequently using legitimate tools, valid accounts and living-off-the-land techniques. The strategic concern is prepositioning for possible disruption during crisis, making water, energy, transportation and communications particularly relevant to New Mexico.

03 · PRC · STATE-LINKED

APT41 / Brass Typhoon

A broad and technically capable ecosystem associated with espionage and historically unusual overlap with financially motivated operations. Its breadth, vulnerability exploitation and supply-chain opportunities make it relevant to public agencies, technology providers, universities and research-adjacent organizations.

04 · PRC · MSS-LINKED

APT40 / Gingham Typhoon

Known for moving quickly against newly disclosed vulnerabilities and targeting organizations with intelligence value. Patch latency is therefore strategic exposure. New Mexico agencies and contractors with internet-facing appliances, remote-access infrastructure and aging platforms should treat rapid remediation as counterintelligence hygiene.

05 · PRC · NATION-STATE

Silk Typhoon

Strategic reconnaissance and compromise activity with emphasis on U.S. targets and technology ecosystems. Cloud identity, service providers and supply chains deserve particular attention because compromise can leap organizational boundaries.

06 · RUSSIA · GRU-LINKED

Forest Blizzard / APT28

A durable espionage actor targeting governments, defense and organizations connected to strategic policy. New Mexico's defense-industrial and research footprint elevates the value of identities, correspondence, travel information and partner relationships.

07 · RUSSIA · SVR-LINKED

Midnight Blizzard / APT29

Strategic espionage with patience and strong identity tradecraft. Its compromise of Microsoft's corporate environment illustrated how legacy identities and seemingly low-value accounts can become routes toward high-value information and downstream access.

08 · RUSSIA · GRU-LINKED

Seashell Blizzard / Sandworm

The disruption actor in this list that should keep infrastructure executives awake. Sandworm has a history of destructive and operationally consequential activity. For New Mexico, segmentation between enterprise IT and operational environments, tested manual operations and recovery engineering are mission requirements.

09 · RUSSIA · FSB-LINKED

Secret Blizzard / Turla

A sophisticated long-duration espionage operation. Recent reporting demonstrated adversary-in-the-middle capability against diplomatic targets, reinforcing the principle that trusted network paths themselves can become hostile terrain.

10 · RUSSIA · STATE-LINKED

Star Blizzard

Persistent spear-phishing and credential theft focused on individuals and organizations with political, defense, research or policy value. Executive staff, counsel, researchers and external affairs personnel can be more valuable targets than servers.

11 · RUSSIA · AFFILIATED

Void Blizzard

A newer espionage cluster observed against government, defense, healthcare, education, IT and transportation. Use of purchased stolen credentials demonstrates why password compromise outside the enterprise can become an enterprise incident.

12 · DPRK · NATION-STATE

Lazarus / TraderTraitor

Blends national objectives with extraordinary revenue-generation operations. The FBI attributed the roughly $1.5B Bybit theft to DPRK TraderTraitor activity in 2025. Beyond crypto, social engineering and developer targeting make technology teams and software supply chains relevant.

13 · DPRK · STATE-DIRECTED INSIDER THREAT

Remote IT Workers

A different attack surface: hiring. FBI reporting describes DPRK workers fraudulently obtaining remote jobs, stealing data and source code, enabling criminal activity and sometimes extorting employers. Identity proofing, contractor governance and privileged-development access become security controls.

14 · IRAN · IRGC-AFFILIATED

CyberAv3ngers

Particularly salient to New Mexico because U.S. water and wastewater systems have been targeted through exposed PLC environments. Internet-exposed OT, default credentials and weak IT/OT boundaries turn small utilities into geopolitical targets.

15 · IRAN · NATION-STATE

APT34 / OilRig-type ecosystem

Iranian espionage operations regularly pursue government and strategic-sector information using credentials, phishing and exploitation. New Mexico exposure increases around public administration, energy, research and organizations tied to federal missions.

16 · CRIMINAL · INDEPENDENT

Scattered Spider / UNC3944

A human-layer predator. Social engineering, help-desk manipulation, identity takeover and aggressive extortion can defeat technically mature environments by attacking process and trust. Google observed targeting expanding across major sectors and into VMware environments.

17 · CRIMINAL · RaaS

Play

A persistent ransomware operation with updated government defensive guidance in 2025. Public-sector entities, healthcare, manufacturing and mid-market organizations remain attractive because downtime creates immediate leverage.

18 · CRIMINAL · RaaS

Medusa

Ransomware and data-extortion activity significant enough for a 2025 joint StopRansomware advisory. For New Mexico organizations, resilience depends less on guessing the brand and more on identity hardening, exposure management, segmentation and recoverable backups.

19 · COMMERCIAL · PSOA

Intellexa / Predator Ecosystem

Represents the private-sector offensive actor problem: sophisticated surveillance capability commercialized for customers. This differs from ordinary cybercrime because the commodity being sold is targeted access and intelligence capability. Executive mobile-device and travel security are the principal local concern.

20 · COMMERCIAL · PSOA

NSO / Pegasus Ecosystem

The archetype of commercial high-end mobile surveillance. Risk is concentrated around specifically selected people rather than indiscriminate enterprise compromise: senior officials, journalists, attorneys, researchers and other intelligence-rich individuals.

New Mexico Impact Analysis

ZIA WOLF ASSESSMENT

The New Mexico problem is not one perimeter. It is an ecosystem. State and local government share telecommunications, cloud, identity, vendors and workforce with national laboratories, defense contractors, utilities, healthcare, universities, tribal entities and private industry. A compromise in one layer can become reconnaissance, access or leverage against another.

Priority defensive moves

1. Treat identity as infrastructure: phishing-resistant MFA for privileged and high-value users, hardened recovery paths, conditional access and aggressive stale-account removal. 2. Inventory internet-facing assets and reduce patch latency, especially edge appliances and remote-access systems. 3. Segment IT from OT and engineer manual operating capability for water and other essential services. 4. Validate telecom and third-party trust boundaries rather than assuming carrier or vendor networks are clean. 5. Exercise ransomware recovery using immutable/offline backups and restoration objectives. 6. Harden help-desk identity verification against Scattered Spider-style social engineering. 7. Strengthen contractor and remote-worker identity proofing, including developer access. 8. Build threat hunting around behaviors and ATT&CK techniques, not actor names alone.

Executive implication

New Mexico should organize cyber defense as a statewide resilience fabric rather than a collection of independent agency firewalls. The strategic objective is shared visibility, rapid intelligence exchange, identity assurance, coordinated incident response and continuity of essential services across public and private dependencies.

Method & Sources

This is an analytic priority list, not an official U.S. government ranking. Categories indicate the best-supported operating model, not a claim that every affiliate or intrusion shares identical sponsorship.
